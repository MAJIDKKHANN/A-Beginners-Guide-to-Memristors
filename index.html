A Beginner’s Guide to Memristors

Memristors and ReRAM explained from zero, with drawings, small experiments you can play with, and no maths you have not seen before.

By Majid Khan

Why I made this

When I started studying memristors, I could not find anything that explained them simply, from the very beginning. Almost everything was a research paper full of equations, written for people who already knew the topic. I had to piece the basic ideas together slowly, one paper and one lecture at a time.

So I wrote the guide I wish I had on day one. Every chapter takes one idea, explains it with a picture and an everyday comparison, and then lets you try it. If you finish all nine chapters, the research papers will start to make sense.

Who it is for

Students in electrical engineering, physics or computer science. Anyone who saw a video about memristors and wants to really understand them. You only need to know that electricity is a flow of electrons, and what a resistor is. Chapter 1 even covers that.

How to read it

Each chapter takes about five minutes. Read them in order, since each one builds on the last.

Look for the green Try it yourself boxes: they are small experiments you can play with right in your browser.
