Word list

Every new word in the book, in plain language, with the chapter where it first appears.

1S1R
One selector, one resistor. A memristor with a selector in front of it to block sneak paths. Chapter 6
1T1R
One transistor, one resistor. A memristor with its own transistor switch. Chapter 6
Bit line
Another name for a column wire in a crossbar. Chapter 6
Compliance current
The maximum current allowed during SET, so the bridge does not grow too thick. Chapter 3
Compute-in-memory
Doing calculations right where the data is stored, instead of moving it to a processor. Chapter 7
Conductance
How easily current flows. The opposite of resistance. Measured in siemens (S). Chapter 7
Crossbar
A grid of row and column wires with a memristor at every crossing. Chapter 6
Electrode
One of the metal layers on either side of the switching layer. Chapter 2
Endurance
How many times a memristor can be rewritten before it wears out. Chapter 5
Error correction
Extra maths that can find and fix a few wrong bits. Chapter 8
Filament
The bridge of oxygen vacancies (or metal atoms) that connects the two electrodes. Chapter 3
Forming
The one-time, bigger push that builds the very first bridge. Chapter 3
HRS
High resistance state. The bridge is broken and the door is closed. Chapter 3
Hysteresis
When the path going up is different from the path coming down, because the device remembers its past. Chapter 4
LRS
Low resistance state. The bridge is complete and the door is open. Chapter 3
MAC (multiply-accumulate)
Multiplying pairs of numbers and adding up the results. The main operation in AI. Chapter 7
Memristor
Memory + resistor. A resistor whose resistance changes with the current that has flowed through it, and stays changed. Chapter 1
Model
A description, usually a formula or netlist, of how a part behaves, so a simulator can use it. Chapter 9
Netlist
A text file listing the parts in a circuit and how they connect. Chapter 9
Memory wall
The slowdown caused by moving data back and forth between memory and processor. Chapter 7
Multi-level cell
Storing more than one bit in a single memristor by using several resistance levels. Chapter 5
Non-volatile
Keeps its data when the power is off. Chapter 5
Oxygen vacancy
A spot in the oxide where an oxygen atom is missing. The stepping stones of the bridge. Chapter 3
Pinched hysteresis loop
The bow-tie shaped current against voltage graph of a memristor. Chapter 4
PUF
Physical unclonable function. A chip fingerprint made from tiny random differences in the hardware. Chapter 8
ReRAM / RRAM
Resistive random-access memory. Memory built from memristors. Chapter 2
RESET
Pushing current backwards to break the bridge. Moves the memristor to HRS. Chapter 3
Resistance
How hard it is for current to flow. Measured in ohms (Ω). Chapter 1
Retention
How long a memristor keeps its data without power. Chapter 5
Selector
A part that only lets current through above a certain voltage. Chapter 6
State variable
The number a model uses to remember where the door is. In our model it is s, from 0 to 1. Chapter 9
SET
Pushing current forwards to complete the bridge. Moves the memristor to LRS. Chapter 3
Sneak path
A detour that current takes through neighbouring memristors in a crossbar. Chapter 6
Switching layer
The thin oxide in the middle of the sandwich, where the bridge forms. Chapter 2
Variability
The natural differences between memristors, and between one switch and the next. Chapter 8
Word line
Another name for a row wire in a crossbar. Chapter 6
