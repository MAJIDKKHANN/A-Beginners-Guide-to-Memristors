Chapter 1 · The big idea

The Resistor That Remembers

A memristor is a doorway for electricity that can slide open or closed, and stays exactly where it is when the power goes off.

First, the plain old resistor

Electricity is a crowd of tiny particles called electrons, all trying to walk down a hallway. A resistor is a narrow doorway in that hallway. The narrower the doorway, the fewer electrons get through. That squeeze is called resistance, and we measure it in ohms (Ω).

Now meet its cousin: the memristor

A memristor (memory + resistor) is a doorway that can slide open or closed. And the thing that moves the door is the electricity itself.

Open door, more electrons, lower resistance. Closed door, fewer electrons, higher resistance. Which way the current flows decides which way the door moves.

The magic trick: it does not forget

Unplug everything and walk away. Come back tomorrow. The door is still exactly where you left it.

The working memory in your laptop (called RAM) forgets everything the moment the power goes away. A memristor does not need power to keep what it knows.

In one sentence

A memristor remembers how much electricity went through it, and in which direction.

Not just a switch. A dimmer.

A light switch has two positions: on and off. A memristor’s door can stop anywhere in between. Each position is a different resistance, and each one stays put.

So one tiny memristor can hold more than a single 0 or 1. We will come back to this in Chapter 5.

How to spot one in a circuit drawing

Engineers draw a resistor as a zigzag. A memristor gets a box with a zigzag inside and a thick bar on one end. The bar shows which way is “open” and which way is “close”, because for a memristor, direction matters.

Did you know?

The memristor was first imagined on paper in 1971, as a missing fourth basic circuit part next to the resistor, the capacitor and the inductor. Nobody built one on purpose and recognised it until 2008.

What we learned

  • A resistor is a doorway with a fixed size.
  • A memristor is a doorway that slides open or closed, depending on which way the current flows.
  • It remembers its position, even with the power off.
  • It can stop in between, like a dimmer.
